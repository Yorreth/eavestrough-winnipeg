Worth a Look

Signs your eavestroughs need attention.

Water overflows during rain

Sheeting over the edge instead of draining through the downspouts usually means a clog, or a trough that’s sized too small for your roof.

Pulling away from the fascia

Gaps, visible hangers, or a section that sags away from the roofline means the fasteners — or the fascia underneath — are failing.

Water pooling near the foundation

If the ground around your foundation stays wet after every rain, your downspouts likely aren’t carrying water far enough away.

Visible sagging, rust, or cracked seams

Older sectional eavestrough develops leaks at the seams over time. A seamless replacement removes those weak points entirely.

Icicles or ice buildup on the roofline

A heavy icicle fringe along the eaves is an early sign of ice damming — worth checking before it starts backing water under your shingles.

Plants or moss growing in the gutters

If there’s enough trapped debris and moisture for something to grow, water isn’t draining the way it should be.
