How Often Should You Clean Your Eavestroughs in Winnipeg?

The generic advice you'll find online — "clean your gutters twice a year" — is a reasonable starting point, but it doesn't account for what actually drives debris buildup in a Winnipeg backyard: the mix of trees around your specific property and what our seasons throw at your roof.

A realistic schedule for Winnipeg homes

SituationRecommended frequency
Few or no nearby treesOnce a year, in fall
Typical residential lot with some treesTwice a year — spring and fall
Mature trees overhanging the roofThree times a year — spring, mid-summer, fall
Evergreens (spruce, pine, cedar) nearbyCheck quarterly — needles clog faster than they look

Why the timing matters here specifically

Spring

Winnipeg's spring melt sends a large volume of water through your eavestrough in a short window. Whatever collected over winter — seeds, buds, wind-blown debris — needs to be out before that melt hits, or you're looking at overflow right at your foundation.

Fall

This is the one homeowners skip most often, and it's the one that matters most. Leaves left in the trough over winter hold moisture against the metal, and a clogged trough going into freeze-up is a major contributor to ice damming — see our guide to ice dams for how that connects.

Mature trees

If your property backs onto mature elms, oaks, or maples, a twice-a-year schedule usually isn't enough. A mid-summer check catches the buildup before it has all season to compact and hold water.

Signs it's overdue: water sheeting over the edge during rain instead of draining through the downspouts, visible plant growth in the trough, or water stains on the siding below the eavestrough. Any of these is worth a look regardless of how long it's been.

Getting it done without the ladder

Cleaning your own eavestrough is possible, but it's also one of the more common sources of ladder falls around the home — and it's easy to miss downspout clogs that aren't visible from the trough itself. A pro visit is quick, checks the whole system including the downspouts, and catches early signs of sagging or seam damage while they're up there.
