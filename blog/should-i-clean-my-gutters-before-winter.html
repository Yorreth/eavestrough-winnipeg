Should I Clean My Gutters Before Winter? A Winnipeg Homeowner's Guide

Every autumn in Winnipeg, the same question comes up for homeowners from River Heights to St. Vital: should I clean my gutters before winter, or is it something that can wait until spring? The short answer is yes — fall eavestrough cleaning is one of the most important maintenance tasks you can do before the temperature drops. Winnipeg winters are unforgiving, and clogged gutters heading into freeze-up can cause thousands of dollars in damage. Here is exactly what you need to know before the snow flies.

Why Winnipeg's Climate Makes Fall Gutter Cleaning Non-Negotiable

Most Canadian cities deal with cold winters, but Winnipeg's combination of extreme temperature swings, heavy freeze-thaw cycles, and significant snowfall creates a specific risk that other markets simply do not face to the same degree. When leaves from mature elms — the kind lining streets in Wolseley, Crescentwood, and Charleswood — pack into your eavestroughs in October and November, water cannot drain freely. That standing water freezes solid once temperatures drop below zero, which in Winnipeg can happen as early as late October.

The result is ice damming. As heat escapes through your roof, the snow above the dam melts, and the meltwater backs up under your shingles. Once it refreezes, it expands and forces water into your attic, wall cavities, and ceiling. Repairs from ice dam damage routinely run into thousands of dollars for Winnipeg homeowners, and most of it is preventable with a single eavestrough cleaning in mid-to-late October, after the bulk of the leaf drop is done.

Should I Clean My Gutters Before Winter or Wait Until Spring?

You should clean your gutters before winter, not after. By the time spring arrives in Winnipeg, gutters that sat clogged all winter have already allowed ice dams to form, may have pulled away from the fascia under the weight of frozen debris and standing ice, and are likely harbouring compressed, decomposing organic material that is difficult to remove and can block downspouts. Cleaning in fall — ideally after most leaves have fallen but before the first hard freeze — protects your home through the entire winter season rather than just addressing the aftermath.

That said, a second light cleaning in early spring is still a good idea. Winnipeg's cottonwood and elm seeds, along with shingle grit washed down during spring rains, can clog downspout strainers quickly. An annual fall clean is the priority; a spring check-up is the bonus.

DIY Eavestrough Cleaning in Winnipeg: Ladder Safety Comes First

If you are comfortable with heights and have a stable ladder, cleaning single-storey eavestroughs yourself is manageable. However, ladder safety in a Manitoba autumn context has a few specific hazards worth understanding before you start.

Ground Conditions

Winnipeg's clay-heavy soil can become soft and uneven after fall rains, making ladder footing unstable. Always use a ladder leveller or standoff stabilizer on soft ground, and avoid working immediately after rainfall. Frost on grass in the early morning — common by mid-October in most Winnipeg neighbourhoods — makes the ground slippery even when it looks fine.

Two-Storey and Higher Homes

For two-storey homes, the risk increases substantially. Working from an extension ladder at heights of 20 feet or more, leaning over a roofline, with wet gloves and uneven autumn ground below is a situation where the math on DIY savings changes quickly. Falls from ladders are among the leading causes of serious injury for homeowners, and Winnipeg's emergency departments see their share of them each fall.

Working safely at heights is a serious consideration. If you are not experienced with ladder work or your home is more than one storey, hiring a licensed professional is strongly recommended. Check with your home insurer about coverage implications for DIY roofline work before you start.

DIY Equipment You Will Need

  • A fibreglass or aluminum extension ladder rated for your weight plus gear
  • A ladder stabilizer to protect your eavestroughs from denting and keep the ladder off the gutters
  • Heavy rubber gloves (not just work gloves — rotting leaves harbour sharp grit and debris)
  • A gutter scoop or garden trowel
  • A bucket or tarp for debris collection
  • A garden hose to flush the system and check downspout flow

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should Winnipeg homeowners clean their eavestroughs?

Most Winnipeg homes benefit from at least one thorough cleaning each fall, after the majority of leaves have dropped in late October or early November. Homes surrounded by mature elms or cottonwoods — common in older neighbourhoods like Wolseley, River Heights, or Elmwood — may also need a second cleaning in spring to clear seed clusters and debris that accumulate after snowmelt.

What happens if I leave clogged gutters through a Winnipeg winter?

Clogged gutters in a Winnipeg winter almost always lead to ice damming, where meltwater backs up under your shingles and refreezes, forcing water into your roof structure, attic, and walls. Beyond interior water damage, the weight of frozen debris and ice can pull eavestrough sections away from the fascia entirely, requiring full replacement in spring. Ignoring fall cleaning routinely costs homeowners far more than the cleaning itself.

Can I clean my eavestroughs from the roof instead of using a ladder?

Walking on a Winnipeg roof in autumn is generally more dangerous than using a proper ladder, not less — fall frost, morning dew, and aging asphalt shingles create a slippery surface with no secure footing. If you want to avoid ladder work entirely, a professional crew with the right equipment is the safer choice rather than attempting rooftop access without fall-arrest gear or roofing experience.
